Chapter One
01 · Military Technology

The Ross Rifle

The Ross Rifle was developed by Sir Charles Ross. Canada wanted to make its own reliable rifle after it could not obtain the license and tools to produce the Lee-Enfield during the Second Boer War. The army adopted the Ross, and production began in 1903.

The rifle was known for its range and accuracy. It won the King’s Prize in 1911 and performed well at the 1913 Bisley Rifle Meeting in England. But target shooting was very different from trench combat. Soldiers found the Ross too long and heavy, and it could jam in the field. In 1916, British commander Sir Douglas Haig ordered Canadian troops to replace it with the Lee-Enfield.

02 · The Explosion · December 6, 1917

The Halifax
Explosion

Halifax was a key wartime port, sending troops, supplies, and ammunition to europe. At 8:45 a.m. on December 6, 1917, the French munitions ship Mont-Blanc collided with the Norwegian relief ship Imo in the harbour. Mont-Blanc was carrying 2,925 tonnes of explosives. It caught fire and drifted toward the waterfront.

At 9:04 a.m., the ship exploded. The blast destroyed buildings across Halifax’s North End and triggered a large tidal wave. About 2,000 people were killed and thousands more were injured. At the time, it was the largest human-made explosion in history, killing about four per cent of Halifax’s population.

03 · The Front Lines · April 9—12, 1917

Vimy Ridge

Vimy Ridge was a high ground in northern France, giving its defenders a view over the surrounding flat land. Germany had turned it into a strong defensive position, with trenches, tunnels, and machine guns.

The Canadian Corps spent the winter of 1916–1917 preparing. Soldiers studied maps, gattered information about the German's defence, and practised on models of the trenches. All four Canadian divisions took part with nearly 100,000 soldiers involved. At 5:30 a.m. on April 9, about 15,000 Canadians began the assault. Most of the ridge had been captured by the end of the day, and the rest was taken by April 12.
