01 · Military Technology

The Ross Rifle

The Ross rifle was a Canadian-designed straight-pull bolt-action rifle adopted by Canada before the First World War. It was prized for its accuracy, but its battlefield service exposed serious problems with reliability and suitability for trench warfare.

Canadian troops used the Ross early in the war. By 1916, after repeated complaints and failures in muddy conditions, Canadian infantry were increasingly equipped with the British Lee-Enfield.

02 · The Home Front · December 6, 1917

The Halifax
Explosion

At 8:45 a.m. on December 6, 1917, the French munitions ship Mont-Blanc collided with the Norwegian vessel Imo in Halifax Harbour. The resulting explosion devastated nearby communities.

About 2,000 people were killed and roughly 9,000 were injured. The disaster destroyed homes, schools, and businesses and left a lasting mark on Halifax.

03 · The Western Front · April 9—12, 1917

Vimy Ridge

On April 9, 1917, the four Canadian divisions fought together as a formation in the Canadian Corps' assault on Vimy Ridge. Canadian forces captured the ridge over the following days.

The victory came at a high human cost: 3,598 Canadians were killed and more than 7,000 were wounded. Vimy later became an important symbol in Canadian remembrance and national identity, although historians continue to debate how much the battle itself changed Canada's relationship with Britain.
